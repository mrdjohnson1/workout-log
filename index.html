Pec Deck, Machine High Row and Assisted Dips swaps

No pec deck machine? The Pec Deck card has a dropdown for Flat DB Bench Chest Fly. Back day rows: Chest-Supported Row every week. Machine High Row on odd weeks and One-Arm DB Row (3 x 8-12 each arm, double progression) on even weeks, in both the Lift and HIIT versions. The Machine High Row card has a dropdown for Seated Cable Row, Incline Bench Cable Pull or Bent-Over Row; each adds 2.5 lb a week, like the high row. Shoulder limiting your range of motion? Both Assisted Dips cards (Chest day and Arms day) have a dropdown for Seated Tricep Pushdown. Each swap keeps the planned sets and reps.

Swaps are picked per week and work in the warm-up week too. Each one has its own starting weight on the Start screen and moves up 5 lb when you hit the top of the reps on every set. The original exercise holds its target while it's swapped out.

Hack Squat swaps

On Hack Squat weeks, a dropdown on the Hack Squat card lets you swap in Smith Machine Squat (4 x 8-12), Goblet Squat (4 x 10-15) or Barbell Landmine Front Squat (4 x 8-12) when the gym has no hack squat machine. In the HIIT version the swap uses the opener's reps.

Each swap has its own starting weight on the Start screen and its own target, built from the last time you did it: +5 lb each time for Smith and Landmine, and +5 lb when you hit 15 reps on every set for Goblet. Hack Squat's own progression picks up where it left off.

How target weights work

Before Week 1: tap Start (left of Wk 1) and enter a starting weight for each lift. It fills Week 1's targets as typed, with no increase. Flat Barbell Press and Leg Press pick theirs up in Week 2, Decline Barbell Press in Week 3. After that, the big red number is your target. Tap it to type your own; tap Reset to go back to automatic. Later weeks build from your number.

Barbell, machine and cable lifts go up every week: +5 lb on lower body lifts and Power Clean, +2.5 lb on upper body and abs. Each target builds from the weight you logged last time, or last time's target if you left it blank.

Rotating lifts build from the last time they were done: Incline, Flat and Decline Barbell Press every 3 weeks; Hack Squat and Leg Press, and Machine High Row and One-Arm DB Row, every 2 weeks.

Switching to the HIIT version

Lift is your main workout. On a short day, tap HIIT (quick) at the top of that day to switch to the circuit version of the same muscle group. Tap Lift (main) to switch back.

HIIT circuit targets are a percentage of each lift's Lift Log target (65% unless you change it on the Start screen), rounded to the nearest 5 lb for dumbbells and 2.5 lb for everything else.

The strength opener uses your full Lift Log weight and counts toward your main progression. Circuit lifts done in the HIIT version hold at their Lift Log target, so they don't increase until you do the full workout again.

HIIT Elliptical stays on the same days in both versions.

Assisted pull-ups, chin-ups, dips and push-ups

Pull-ups, chin-ups and dips use the assisted machine, so the number you log is the assist weight: more assist is easier. Enter your starting assist on the Start screen. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, the next target drops 5 lb. Once it reaches 0 you're doing them at full body weight.

In the HIIT version, the app adds assist so you lift about the same percentage of your working load as the other circuit lifts. It needs a body weight on the Body tab for that; without one it uses your Lift assist.

Push-ups: pick Smith Machine or Hammer Strength Rack on the card, then choose the Level (5 to 0) where you set the bar in place of a weight. Move one level when you hit the top of the reps on every set.

HIIT cardio rotation

Your 3 weekly HIIT sessions use one machine per week on a 3-week loop: Week 1 elliptical, Week 2 Stair Master, Week 3 treadmill run, then it starts over.

The elliptical has levels too. Advanced is your original 20-minute elliptical routine (Resistance 5 to 14, Incline 5 to 11); the levels below it lower the resistance and incline: Beginner peaks at Resistance 10, Incline 7; Beginner-Intermediate at 11 and 8; Intermediate at 12 and 9; Intermediate-Advanced at 13 and 10.

Every elliptical workout has an Elliptical type dropdown. Pick Resistance only when the machine has no incline: the app moves the incline work into resistance, adding 1 resistance level for every 2 incline steps above 5 (the hill push becomes a heavy push), up to 20. Your choice is saved for that day and becomes the default for the next elliptical workout.

The Stair Master and treadmill follow the same 20-minute pattern as your elliptical workout: 2 minutes recover, 1 minute push, with the settings climbing to a peak at minutes 12 to 15 and then tapering off to the finish. On the treadmill, elliptical resistance becomes speed and elliptical incline becomes treadmill incline. The Stair Master has no incline, only speed levels, so each 2-step rise in elliptical incline becomes 1 level higher, and each push adds levels on top of that. Steps per minute are an average (about 24 at Level 1, plus about 7 per level); your machine may differ a little.

Each machine steps up a level every time it comes around: Beginner, Beginner-Intermediate, Intermediate, Intermediate-Advanced, then Advanced every time after that. From Week 17 on, every Advanced elliptical, Stair Master and treadmill turn becomes Advanced+. Elliptical Advanced+ follows the same timing: pushes at Resistance 12 to 16 (one is a hill push at Incline 13), two 30-second sprints at Resistance 14 with an all-out stride rate, and recoveries at Resistance 5 to 7. Stair Master Advanced+ is your 20-minute routine with a 3-minute warm-up, 5 one-minute pushes at Levels 12 to 18 with 90-second recoveries, two 30-second all-out sprints at Levels 16 to 20, and a 2-minute cool-down. Treadmill Advanced+ follows the same timing: pushes at 6.5 to 7.5 mph (one is a 5% hill push at 6.0 mph), two 30-second sprints at 8.0 to 8.5 mph, and walking recoveries at 3.5 to 3.8 mph. Start speeding up about 10 seconds before each push, since the belt takes time to change speed. Treadmill speeds show minutes per mile first, with mph in parentheses. Machine levels and speeds are starting points: adjust so the push minutes feel hard and the recover minutes feel easy, and slow down if your knees or shins complain.

Benchmark test

On the Saturday at the end of each 12-week block (first one Feb 20, 2027), a benchmark test replaces the ride: max pull-ups, 2-minute push-ups, 2-minute sit-ups, a max plank and a 1.5-mile run. Each result is compared with the Navy SEAL PST minimum, the Air Force Pararescue (PJ) minimum and competitive scores, and with your last block. Results are on the Body tab.

Water and Electrolytes

Every day has a water section, including the warm-up week. It sits at the bottom of each day. Type the ounces you drank and tap Add water. The bar fills toward 128 oz (1 gallon), the plan's goal; change it on the Start screen.

For a flavored or electrolyte drink, pick the drink and flavor, enter how much you used and tap Add drink. Sodium, calories, carbs and caffeine count by the amount used, per serving: 1 scoop of POV is 750 mg sodium whether you mix it in 12 oz or 32 oz. Servings are scoops (POV), squeezes (MiO: 1/2 tsp for Original, 3/4 tsp for Hydrate and Energy), packets, sticks or tablets. Gatorade Thirst Quencher powder counts by ounces mixed to label strength, since its label is per 12 oz. Water ounces are optional and only count toward your water. Where the plan gives a range, the high end counts.

Each entry shows in the log with the time you added it; tap Remove to undo one. Sodium is tracked against a 1,500 mg daily limit (change it on the Start screen) from your drinks. The yellow box warns you when you're within 20% of the limit or over it, when you log more than 1 electrolyte drink, and for caffeine and full-sugar drinks.

Body weight and extra cardio

Weigh in once a week on Monday (there's a box at the top of each Monday) or on the Body tab. The Body tab shows your trend from your last 4 weigh-ins, your goal range as a shaded band, and about when you'll reach it at your current pace.

The target is losing 0.5% to 1% of body weight per week. Each weigh-in sets the next week's extra cardio automatically. Below the target, it adds 10 minutes of incline walking on your 2 non-HIIT days (up to 20). Faster than the target, it cuts 10 minutes, or suggests eating a little more if there's no extra cardio to cut. After a change it waits 3 weeks before changing again. The amount shows on those days, on each Monday, and next to each week on the Body tab.

The app decides between none, 10 and 20 minutes on its own; there is nothing to set. Your lifting never changes based on body weight; keeping the weights heavy is what protects muscle while you lose fat.

Double progression (dumbbell lifts)

Add reps first, then weight. Dumbbells usually jump 5 lb at a time, which is a big jump on lighter lifts (20 to 25 lb is 25%).

Stay at the same weight until you reach the top of the rep range on every set. Then the target goes up 5 lb and you start back at the bottom of the range.

Log the reps from your weakest set, so the weight only goes up once every set hits the top.

Example, 3 x 10-12: 40 lb for 10, then 11, then 12. Next session the target is 45 lb.

Back up your log

Your log is saved only on this phone. Save a backup every week or two, and before deleting the app or clearing Safari data. Either file type restores everything, including which days you switched to HIIT; the .csv also opens in Google Sheets or Excel. Lift Log backups restore here too. Saving opens your device's save or share options: on iPhone choose Save to Files (or Google Drive, Dropbox and similar), on Android pick Drive or Files.

To restore, tap Restore from backup and browse to the .json or .csv file you saved (Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive or Downloads). This replaces everything currently in the app.
